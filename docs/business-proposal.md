## 1. Problem framing

Triage is a one-time snapshot taken on a patient who is still changing.

An emergency department rarely lacks equipment — it lacks sequenced attention. A
nurse must rank strangers in minutes, and that ranking is then frozen while
patients keep deteriorating in the waiting room.

The evidence is unambiguous:

| | |
|---|---|
| **32.2%** of 5,315,176 adult ED encounters were mistriaged under ESI; sensitivity for high-acuity illness 65.9% | Sax et al., *JAMA Network Open*, 2023 |
| **1 in 82** patients delayed 6–8 hours from arrival dies as a result; 30-day mortality rises linearly past 5 hours | Jones et al., *EMJ*, 2022 |
| **+1.8%** absolute mortality per hour of delayed antibiotics in septic shock | Liu et al., *AJRCCM*, 2017 |
| **12.7%** of confirmed heart attacks present without chest pain (16.2% of women); 5-year mortality HR 2.12 | Björck et al., *Open Heart*, 2018 |

So the design question is not *"who is sickest now?"* but **"who is becoming
sickest while nobody is looking?"**

## 2. Solution design

Four layers, and one invariant that holds by construction.

| Layer | Role | Decides or recommends |
|---|---|---|
| **0 · Red-flag gate** | Ten cited hard rules, age-banded, no model, runs offline | **Decides** |
| **1 · Acuity scorer** | Gradient-boosted trees + Mondrian conformal confidence | Recommends |
| **2 · Dynamic re-ranker** | Trajectory over trailing windows, shock index, queue aging | Re-orders |
| **3 · Human authority** | Sign-off, override, hash-chained audit | **Decides** |

**The invariant, as code** — five lines, verifiable by a judge in the time it
takes to read:

```python
def apply(current, proposed, source):
    if source is Source.HUMAN:
        return proposed            # nurse authority is absolute
    return min(current, proposed)  # machines escalate, never relent
```

### Layer 0 answers three things, not two

This distinction is what keeps the gate usable, and we learned it by getting it
wrong first — blanket worst-case imputation red-flagged nearly every patient,
which is precisely the alert fatigue the system exists to prevent.

- **Confirmed** — a rule fired on values someone actually recorded. Band 1.
- **Cannot rule out** — the rule fires only when a missing vital is assumed
  worst-case. Band 2, plus an instruction to measure it. Unknown is not normal,
  but it is not the same as critical.
- **Not evaluable** — the source has no such column at all. No dataset here
  carries GCS; gating every patient on an assumed GCS of 3 fires that rule on
  all of them.

### What the system decides, and what a human decides

| System decides | Human decides |
|---|---|
| Firing red-flag alerts | Final acuity sign-off |
| Ordering within one band | Any downgrade of a flag |
| Re-assessment timers | Disposition and discharge |
| Flagging missing data | Triage under true scarcity |

## 3. Target users

- **Primary — the triage nurse**, mid-shift, managing several patients at once.
  The board answers one question: who should I see next, and has that changed?
- **Secondary — the charge nurse**, during surge, and the **clinical governance
  lead**, reading the audit trail after the fact.
- **Explicitly not** physicians making treatment decisions. ATRIA changes the
  order of attention, never clinical care. That boundary is what makes it
  deployable inside existing accountability structures.

## 4. Evidence and impact

Full tables in [`results.md`](results.md). Headlines:

**Layer 2 discriminates on real patients.** Across 159 real MIMIC-IV-ED stays
with repeated vitals, replayed reading by reading, the re-ranker flagged **32.2%
of patients later admitted or transferred against 12.2% of those discharged
home**, a **median 164 minutes** before their last recorded reading.

**Confidence is a guarantee, not a heuristic.** Mondrian class-conditional
conformal calibration reaches **≥95% coverage on each class separately**,
including the rare critical class that marginal conformal under-covers.

**The fairness gap was real, and closing it worked.** Geriatric patients were
undertriaged at **18.1%** while children were never missed. Subgroup-conditional
calibration cut the sensitivity gap to **0.6%**, all groups above the 95% target.
The cost is stated: geriatric false alarms rose, paediatric false alarms fell.

**Latency has headroom.** Worst p95 is **41 ms under 3× surge**, against the
400 ms budget on the solution slide.

### Three findings a competing prototype would have missed

1. **The Isfahan dataset encodes its own triage decision.** 100% of grade-1
   patients have zero recorded vitals against 0.1% of grade-3 — the sickest
   bypass the triage form entirely. A model using missing-indicators would have
   scored near-perfectly on hospital workflow rather than physiology. We detected
   it, proved what it would have done, and excluded the dataset from training.
2. **Our model had learned that missing vitals are reassuring.** Blanking heart
   rate, respiratory rate or systolic *lowered* predicted risk — three silent
   undertriage paths in a system that promises the opposite. Found by auditing,
   not assuming; now clamped.
3. **Our own Layer 0 was adult-calibrated.** It fired hypotension on a
   3-year-old with a systolic of 88, which is normal at that age — exactly the
   failure the brief names. Thresholds are now age-banded on PALS.

## 5. Phased roadmap

Each phase is gated on a measured threshold, not a date.

| Phase | What runs | Gate to advance |
|---|---|---|
| **1 · Shadow** | Runs beside triage, recommends nothing, logs everything | Agreement with clinician judgement measured; would-be lead time quantified on local data |
| **2 · Advisory** | Recommendations surfaced; every action a nurse decision | Critical undertriage below local baseline; override rate stable; no subgroup gap above 5 points |
| **3 · Integrated** | Writes priority to the EHR track board (e.g. Epic ASAP) | Sustained performance through one full recalibration cycle |

Shadow mode is not a formality. It is how a system earns the right to be trusted
by staff who have seen tools fail before, and it costs nothing clinically because
it touches no decision.

## 6. Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| **Alert fatigue** — over-flagging trains staff to ignore the board | Operating point chosen on a published cost curve, not a default. Layer 0's three-way answer keeps unmeasured vitals out of band 1 |
| **Automation bias** — staff defer to the score | Confidence and missing fields shown on every row by default; the interface never presents a bare number |
| **Calibration drift across sites** | Per-site recalibration in onboarding; monitored continuously. Cross-site evaluation is built into `eval/cross_site.py` |
| **Subgroup harm** | Audited every run, mitigated by subgroup-conditional calibration, reported before and after |
| **Liability and contestability** | Hash-chained append-only audit; every decision reconstructable including model and rule versions |
| **Scope creep into diagnosis** | Refused by design. ATRIA orders attention; it does not suggest conditions or treatment |

## 7. Assumptions and limitations

Stated here in one place, deliberately, rather than left for a reviewer to find.

- **Jurisdiction assumed: HIPAA (US)**, matching the provenance of the datasets
  used. This drives retention, consent and audit-trail design.
- **The outcome label is a proxy.** Yale's is hospital admission; the synthetic
  generator's is physiological deterioration. Neither is ICU-transfer-or-death,
  which no open ED dataset supplies with timestamps. This is the first thing we
  would fix with real hospital access.
- **Layer 1 currently trains on synthetic patients** calibrated to real priors.
  Yale's 560,486 real visits are downloaded but await a one-off R extraction.
- **Layer 2 is validated on 159 real trajectories** — real, but a small sample.
- **Paediatric cases are synthetic.** The adult-skewed real sources cannot
  supply them, and we would not claim paediatric performance from them.
- **Fairness is audited on sex and age band** until Yale's race, ethnicity,
  language and insurance columns are available.
